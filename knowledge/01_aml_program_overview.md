# Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Program Overview

## Purpose
The Bank's AML program is designed to detect, prevent, and report money laundering and terrorist financing activity across all lines of business, including consumer banking, commercial banking, payments, and wealth management. It exists to satisfy obligations under the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) and related regulations, and to protect the institution from being used as a conduit for illicit funds.

## Four Pillars
1. **Internal controls** — policies, procedures, and systems that ensure ongoing compliance, including transaction monitoring thresholds and escalation paths.
2. **Independent testing** — periodic audits performed by a party independent of day-to-day AML operations, validating that controls operate as designed.
3. **BSA/AML Officer** — a designated individual with sufficient authority and resources responsible for the day-to-day management of the program.
4. **Training** — ongoing, role-based AML training for employees, with additional depth for staff in higher-risk functions (e.g. private banking, correspondent banking).

## New Product and Change Risk Considerations
Any new product, feature, vendor relationship, geography, or customer segment must be evaluated for money-laundering typologies before launch. Reviewers should consider:
- Whether the change introduces new **funds transfer mechanisms** (e.g. instant payments, cross-border rails) that could be exploited for layering or rapid movement of funds.
- Whether existing transaction monitoring scenarios and thresholds adequately cover the new activity pattern, or whether new scenarios must be built before launch.
- Whether the change affects **customer onboarding** in a way that could weaken identity verification or due diligence controls.
- Whether the change increases exposure to **higher-risk customer segments** (e.g. money services businesses, cash-intensive businesses, politically exposed persons).

## Escalation
Any change assessed as introducing a material AML control gap must be escalated to the BSA/AML Officer and documented in the change's risk assessment record before implementation, with either a remediation plan or an accepted-risk sign-off from the appropriate committee.
