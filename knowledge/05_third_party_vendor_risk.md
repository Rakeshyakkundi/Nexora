# Third-Party and Vendor Risk

## Scope
Any vendor, fintech partner, or third party that originates transactions, handles customer data, performs onboarding on the Bank's behalf, or provides a financial-crime-relevant control (e.g. outsourced screening) falls under third-party risk requirements.

## Due Diligence Expectations
Before onboarding a new vendor, the Bank must assess:
- The vendor's own AML/sanctions compliance program maturity, including whether they are regulated and by whom.
- Data handling and security practices for any customer or transaction data shared with the vendor.
- Whether the vendor performs any compliance function (screening, monitoring, identity verification) on the Bank's behalf, and if so, how that performance is validated on an ongoing basis.
- Contractual rights for the Bank to audit the vendor and to terminate the relationship if compliance standards are not met.

## Ongoing Monitoring
Vendor risk is not a one-time assessment. The Bank must periodically re-assess vendors, particularly when:
- The vendor's role expands (e.g. from data processing to transaction origination).
- The vendor experiences a security incident or regulatory action.
- The underlying regulatory environment changes in the vendor's jurisdiction.

## Relevance to Change Assessments
When a proposed change involves a new or expanded vendor relationship, the assessment should document:
1. What specific function the vendor performs, and whether it is compliance-relevant.
2. Whether the vendor's controls have been independently validated (e.g. SOC 2, regulatory attestation) or only self-attested.
3. Data flow between the Bank and vendor, including what happens to data if the relationship terminates.
4. Fallback plan if the vendor's control fails (e.g. what happens if outsourced screening misses a match).
